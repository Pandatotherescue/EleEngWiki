Marine electronics divides fairly cleanly by vessel class. SOLAS-regulated commercial shipping is served by a handful of Japanese and European makers who supply complete type-approved GMDSS installations. Leisure and small commercial craft are served by a different, larger group competing on price and features.

## Commercial and SOLAS

**JRC (Japan Radio Co.)** — Founded 1915, one of the two big Japanese marine houses alongside [Furuno](/equipment/furuno-marine). Supplies complete bridge systems: radar, ECDIS, GMDSS and satellite.

| Model | Role |
| --- | --- |
| JSS-2150 / 2250 / 2500 | MF/HF radio equipment, 150 W / 250 W / 500 W |
| JHS-800S | VHF radiotelephone with DSC |
| JUE series | Inmarsat terminals |
| NCR-333 | NAVTEX receiver |

**Jotron** — Norwegian, and the name behind the **Tron** brand of distress beacons. Tron EPIRBs and AIS-SARTs appear in other manufacturers' catalogues, including Furuno's, because Jotron supplies them for rebadging. Also makes GMDSS survival-craft handhelds (the TR-30 series) and air traffic control radio.

**Sailor** equipment is covered under [Cobham SATCOM](/equipment/cobham-satcom); **Lars Thrane** under its [own page](/equipment/lars-thrane).

## Distress beacons

A distinct specialism, because a beacon is a device that must work once, years after installation, having never been switched on.

| Maker | Products |
| --- | --- |
| Ocean Signal (UK) | rescueME EPIRB1 / EPIRB3, PLB1, MOB1 AIS beacon |
| ACR Electronics (US) | GlobalFix EPIRBs, ResQLink PLBs |
| McMurdo (UK) | SmartFind EPIRBs and AIS beacons |
| Jotron (NO) | Tron EPIRBs, AIS-SART |

All transmit on **406 MHz** to the Cospas-Sarsat constellation, with a 121.5 MHz homing signal, and increasingly an AIS transmitter so nearby vessels can home in without waiting for the satellite alert to route through a rescue coordination centre. Ocean Signal and McMurdo are both part of the Drew Marine group; ACR and Ocean Signal have also shared ownership, so the apparent variety in this market is narrower than the brand count suggests.

## Leisure and small commercial

**Icom** — Japanese, and the most consistently well-regarded marine VHF maker. The IC-M range covers fixed and handheld, and the IC-M803/M804 is the standard small-vessel HF SSB set. Icom also builds land mobile and amateur equipment, which is unusual breadth.

**Standard Horizon** — the marine brand of Yaesu, now part of Motorola Solutions. The GX fixed-mount and HX handheld ranges are widely fitted, and generally give more features per unit of cost than Icom at the expense of some polish.

**Navico** — the group holding **Simrad**, **B&G** and **Lowrance**, now owned by Brunswick. Radios such as the Simrad RS40 and B&G V60 share hardware across the brands, differentiated by which chartplotter ecosystem they integrate with.

**Garmin** — entered marine from aviation and handheld GPS, and competes on integration with its own chartplotters rather than on radio performance. Acquired **Vesper Marine**, whose Cortex unit combines VHF, AIS and vessel monitoring in one device.

**em-trak** — a UK specialist in AIS transponders rather than a full-line maker, supplying both its own brand and OEM units to others.

## What the split means in practice

Commercial equipment is bought for type approval, service network and survey history; leisure equipment for features and price. That produces two quite different design cultures from the same underlying technology.

A SOLAS VHF set and a leisure VHF set do the same radio job to similar RF performance. The commercial one costs several times more because it carries type approval against IEC 60945, a duplicated power arrangement, a documented failure mode, and a service organisation obliged to keep it working for the vessel's life. None of that shows up in a specification comparison, which is why such comparisons mislead across the boundary.
